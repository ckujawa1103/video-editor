# THE WOMAN BEYOND THE HILL
### Pilot episode — *THE OLD ACCOUNTS*

**Runtime target:** 6:30–7:00
**Format:** Narrated folk-horror. No lip-sync dialogue. Voice carries; images support.

---

## SERIES PREMISE

An anthology of short horror films drawn from recovered accounts of an
unnamed ancient people. Each episode is self-contained. No connective
mythology, no framing device, no host.

**The decoupling rule:** no proper nouns from any real religion, no place
names that can be looked up, no divine name. Characters are titles — the
king, the woman, the old man, the two servants. A viewer with no background
sees folk horror. A viewer who knows the source sees it in the second
minute and tells someone.

**What we keep from the source:** the beat structure, the moral logic, and
the specific cruelty of the ending. That is where the parallel lives. Change
the names, never the shape.

---

## WHAT CHANGED FROM THE SOURCE

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| Named king | **the king** — never named |
| Named prophet | **the old man** |
| Named nation, named enemy | **the kingdom**, **the army from the north** |
| Named locations | **the ridge**, **the far side of the hill** |
| The LORD | **the voice that had answered him** |
| Priestly divination methods | **the three ways of asking** |
| Explicit scripture cadence | archaic-but-invented narration voice |

The last row is the real work. Rules for the narration voice:

- No contractions, ever.
- Short declaratives joined by *and*. Let sentences accumulate rather than subordinate.
- Concrete nouns. No abstractions except *silence*, *answer*, *fear*.
- Never explain a feeling. State the action and stop.
- Archaic word order sparingly — one inversion per paragraph at most. Overdone, it reads as parody.

---

## VISUAL BIBLE

Unchanged from the original treatment. Lock before generating.

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Palette** | Bone white, dried blood, deep umber, cold blue-grey night. Nothing saturated. |
| **Light** | Single-source. Lamp, moon, or fire. Hard falloff to black. |
| **Camera** | Locked or near-locked. Slow push at most. No orbits, no drone, no handheld. |
| **Faces** | Avoided. Backs, silhouettes, hands, mouths in shadow, figures at distance. |
| **The old man** | Never rendered. A displacement at the edge of lamplight. The woman describes; we do not show. |
| **Costume** | Non-specific ancient. Undyed wool, leather, bronze. **No period or regional markers that place it.** |
| **Texture** | Heavy grain. Dust. Cloth, stone, ash. Nothing clean. |
| **Aspect** | 2.39:1 |

**Sound:** Sub-bass drone. Wind. Cloth. No conventional score. At least 45 seconds of the runtime carries no audio at all.

### I. THE SILENCE

**[V]** Black. Four seconds.

**[N]**
The old man was dead. They had buried him in his own town, and the kingdom had mourned him, and then the kingdom had gone on.

**[V]** SHOT 1 — Wide. A stone cairn on a hillside at dusk. No figures. Dry grass moving. Locked.

**[N]**
And the king had driven the speakers-to-the-dead out of his land. All of them. It had been his first law.

**[V]** SHOT 2 — Close. A doorway sealed with stones. The mortar is not old.

**[S]** Wind rises.

**[V]** SHOT 3 — Wide. An encampment across a valley, seen from very far. Hundreds of fires in blue dark. Too many.

**[N]**
In the autumn the army came down from the north and made its camp in the valley, and the king brought his own men up onto the ridge, and from the ridge he counted their fires.

**[V]** SHOT 4 — A single figure on the ridge, back to camera, small against those fires. Motionless.

**[N]**
He counted them twice. Then he stopped counting.

**[V]** SHOT 5 — Extreme close. A hand on stone. Knuckles pale. Hold.

**[N]**
There were three ways of asking, and he had used all of them. He asked in the way of sleep, and slept without dreaming. He asked in the way of the lots, and the lots fell without meaning. He asked in the way of the speakers, and no speaker spoke.

**[V]** SHOT 6 — Interior tent. A man kneeling, seen from behind. Lamp guttering. Hold six seconds. Nothing happens.

**[N]**
The voice that had answered him all his life did not answer him now.

**[S]** All audio drops. Three seconds of nothing.

### II. THE ROAD

**[N]**
And so the king said to his servants: find me a woman who speaks to the dead.

**[V]** SHOT 7 — Two servants in a doorway, faces in shadow, unmoving. They have understood what was asked.

**[N]**
They did not ask him whether he remembered his own law. They said: there is one. She is beyond the hill.

**[V]** SHOT 8 — Wide. Dark hills. A single distant point of light.

**[N]**
He took off the cloth that marked him as king, and put on the cloth of a man who was no one.

**[V]** SHOT 9 — Close. Fine cloth folded and set down on a chest.

**[V]** SHOT 10 — Close. Rough cloth pulled over a shoulder.

**[N]**
And he went out at night, and two men went with him.

**[V]** SHOT 11 — Wide. Three figures on a dark road, moonlit, walking away from camera. Long hold. Let them get small.

**[S]** Footsteps. Wind.

### III. THE BARGAIN

**[V]** SHOT 12 — Interior. Low room. One lamp. A woman seated, face away from the light. A figure fills the doorway, backlit, featureless.

**[N]**
He said to her: speak to the dead for me. Bring up the one I name.

**[V]** SHOT 13 — Close. The woman's hands, still in her lap. They do not move.

**[N]**
And the woman said: you know what the king has done. You know he has cut us out of the land like rot out of a beam. Why do you set a trap for my life?

**[V]** SHOT 14 — The sealed doorway from Shot 2. One second. Cut away.

**[N]**
And he swore to her that no harm would come to her for this thing. He swore it on the voice that no longer answered him. He did not tell her that part.

**[V]** SHOT 15 — Close. The lamp flame. Still. Then leaning.

**[S]** Drone begins. Below hearing.

**[N]**
And she said: who?

**[V]** SHOT 16 — Wide. Both figures. Distance between them.

**[N]**
And he named the old man.

**[S]** Cut to silence.

**[V]** SHOT 17 — The floor. Packed earth. Hold. The shadow is wrong and you cannot say how.

**[V]** SHOT 18 — The lamp flame bends away from nothing.

**[V]** SHOT 19 — Close on the woman. Her face, for the first time. Her eyes are open too wide.

**[N]**

### V. THE ANSWER

**[N]**
And the old man said: why have you troubled me. I was not resting, but I was not here.

**[V]** SHOT 24 — Hold on the dark past the lamp. The voice comes from there. Never cut to a source.

**[N]**
And the king said: the army is in the valley and I cannot count them. The voice has gone from me. It does not answer in sleep and it does not answer in the lots and there is no one left to ask. Tell me what to do.

**[V]** SHOT 25 — Close. The king's hands flat on the earth. Trembling.

**[N]**
And the old man said: why do you ask me. You are asking the wrong question. You want to know what to do and there is nothing to do.

**[S]** Drone rises. Barely.

**[N]**
The kingdom was taken out of your hand a long time ago. It was taken the day you decided the law was for other men. You have been king of nothing for years and no one told you.

**[V]** SHOT 26 — The lamp. Guttering.

**[N]**
Tomorrow the army will come up the ridge, and your men will break, and it will be finished before the sun is high.

**[V]** SHOT 27 — Slow push toward the dark corner. Nothing is there. Keep pushing.

**[N]**
And tomorrow night you will be where I am. You, and your sons with you.

**[S]** Cut. Absolute silence.

**[V]** SHOT 28 — Black. Three full seconds.

### VI. THE MEAL

**[N]**
The king fell down full length on the floor and could not get up. He had not eaten that day, or the night before it.

**[V]** SHOT 29 — Wide. The king full-length on the floor. The woman standing over him. Neither moves.

**[N]**
And the woman looked at him for a while. She had put her life in her hands for him and he had lied to her and now he was on her floor and he was going to die tomorrow.

**[V]** SHOT 30 — Close. Hands kneading dough. Ordinary. Almost unbearable after what came before.

**[N]**
And she said: get up. Eat something first.

**[V]** SHOT 31 — Bread on a plain dish. Steam. The most human image in the film.

**[N]**
And she killed the calf she had been keeping, and ground the flour, and baked the bread herself, and set it in front of him and in front of his two men. And they ate it.

**[V]** SHOT 32 — Three figures at a low table, seen from distance through a doorway. Eating in silence. Long hold. Nobody speaks.

**[N]**
Nobody said anything. There was nothing that would have been worth saying.

**[V]** SHOT 33 — The dark road again. Three figures walking toward camera, past it, out of frame. The road stays empty.

**[S]** Wind. Then nothing.

**[N]**
And they got up, and went out into the night, and went back to the ridge.

**[V]** SHOT 34 — Wide. The ridge at first light. Empty. Hold five seconds.

**[N]**
*(flat, unhurried)*
It happened the way the old man said it would.

**[V]** Cut to black.

**CARD:** THE OLD ACCOUNTS

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Shot count:** 34, unchanged. Visual plan is identical to the source version — only the words changed.
- **The strongest addition is in Section V.** "You have been king of nothing for years and no one told you" has no equivalent in the source. Decoupling gave you room to write a better line than adaptation allowed. Look for that room in every episode.
- **The woman gets more interiority here** (Shot 30 narration). The source gives her none. This is the main creative gain from going non-literal.
- **Never render the old man.** Discard any take showing a figure in the dark, however good.
- Generate 4–6 takes per shot. Only shots 19 and 23 involve a face.
- At least 45 seconds with no audio.

## SERIES SLATE

Same treatment — keep the shape, drop every proper noun.

1. **The Left-Handed Man** — a tribute-bearer, a fat ruler, a locked upper room, a hidden blade. *(Judges 3)*
2. **What He Promised** — a commander swears to sacrifice the first thing that comes out of his door to greet him. His daughter comes out. *(Judges 11)*
3. **The Children and the Bears** — a holy man is mocked by children on a road. Two verses long. No resolution, no moral, nothing explained. *(2 Kings 2)*
4. **The Last Night** — one household, one night, something moving through a city killing the firstborn. Told entirely from inside the house. *(Exodus 12)*

Number 3 is the strongest of the four, because the absence of any moral is the horror, and stripped of scriptural context it becomes pure folk dread.
